# Alpha -- Timeline

**What he is:** Son of a living nexus and a goddess. Human-shaped. Not human-capped. Numbers and skills below are what eighteen years of lived life made visible -- snapshots, not ceilings. Balance him with Nexus Absorption and energy flow, not metatype math.

**Setting notes for consistency:**
The circle lives entirely off the grid, meaning no commlinks, no cyberware, no wireless devices of any kind exist in Alpha's early environment by choice, not by poverty. This is why his passive energy discharges go unnoticed for so long -- there is simply nothing electronic nearby to visibly malfunction until he leaves the deep wilderness. His growing sense of "inward" and "outward" energy corresponds to adept expression (channeled into himself) and magician expression (projected outward as spells), which as a Mystic Adept he does both, though as a child he has no formal names for either. Astral perception is something he grows into rather than wakes up owning; his Assensing sense stays crude and instinctive for years. His Way, whatever he eventually calls it, is built around this inward/outward duality and his identity as what he is -- nexus-blood, goddess-blood -- rather than around any single school of combat or craft.

## Year 1
A druidic circle living off the grid in the deep forest of the Olympic Peninsula found a newborn at the base of an ancient cedar, his hair glowing faintly and his eyes shifting through colors that had no clear cause. Every awakened member of the circle felt what he was the moment they came close -- not a SURGE accident, not an ordinary infant with strange gifts, but the son of a living nexus and a goddess wearing a child's shape -- and rather than fear it or turn away they simply took him in. They named him Alpha and raised him honestly from the very beginning, telling him what they understood about him and admitting openly what they did not. Even as an infant he was unusually strong for his size, gripping and pulling himself upright earlier than the circle's own children ever had.

**Skills:** None. Infant.

**Innate, present from birth:**
- Bioluminescence -- his hair glows faintly
- Mood Eyes -- his eyes shift color with emotional state
- Low Pain Tolerance -- despite his physical strength, his young body has no real capacity to endure pain yet; wound penalties will hit him earlier than they should for someone his size
- Uncouth -- raised entirely within an isolated circle with no exposure to the wider world's social norms, he has no real frame of reference for how people outside his own small community think or behave

**Starting condition of his upbringing, not innate:**
- Uneducated -- born into a community with no schools, no formal curriculum, and no access to any Sixth World academic system. Whatever he comes to know, he will have to learn entirely through lived experience and whoever chooses to personally teach him.

**Physical development:** Notably strong grip and early muscle control for an infant.

## Year 2
Long before he had words for any of it, he was already perceiving the world in ways an ordinary child would not. He felt electrical currents and the pull of magnetic fields the way other infants feel warmth or hunger, and the land around the circle's camp responded subtly to his presence, water seeming to run toward him, fires burning a little steadier when he was near. Animals kept a wary distance from him even at this age. The circle didn't try to explain any of it away. They simply accepted it as part of who he was. Physically he was walking and climbing well ahead of schedule, already testing his balance on fallen logs and low branches. The circle sang to him constantly in these early years, old work songs and lullabies passed down from whichever traditions each member had carried into the group before joining it.

**Skills:** None. Pre-verbal.

**Innate, present from birth, first observable this year:**
- Electrosense -- feels electrical currents without effort
- Magnetoception -- feels the pull of magnetic fields
- Critter Spook -- animals keep a wary distance, reacting instinctively to the astral disturbance his Astral Hazing radiates outward, long before anyone understood what was actually causing it

**Physical development:** Early walking and climbing, strong balance for his age.

## Year 3
He began to speak, learning English alongside the circle's older druidic vocabulary for describing natural forces, and the two blended together in his mind as a single way of understanding the world, taking to both languages equally rather than treating one as primary. His eyes continued to shift color with his moods, and rather than treat this as something to hide, the circle talked about it openly, each person sharing what a given color meant to them without any embarrassment. He absorbed everything around him hungrily and asked more questions than anyone in the circle could ever fully answer. His body kept pace with his curiosity, and he was already running short distances and climbing small trees without help. He also started learning to identify birdsong, matching calls to the birds that made them, and began picking out simple shapes and patterns burned or carved into the camp's tools. Around the evening fires he started humming along to the circle's songs, and one of the older members began teaching him simple rhythms on a hand drum.

**Skills gained:**
- English (Native)
- Interest: Natural World 1
- Gymnastics 1
- Interest: Birdsong/Wildlife Sounds 1
- Performance (Hand Drum) 1

**Physical development:** Running and basic climbing without assistance.

## Year 4
He started following the older members of the circle wherever they went, moving through the underbrush quietly without anyone having taught him how. It was clear his body already understood how to exist in a forest most people would find disorienting. Around this time he began mentioning that he could feel things others apparently couldn't, the distant hum of a power line miles away, the constant pull of magnetic north regardless of which direction he faced. He assumed at first that everyone experienced the world this way. When he brought it up, Bracken listened far more carefully than the offhand comment seemed to warrant and asked him to describe exactly what he was feeling. His strength and endurance were both noticeably ahead of the other children his age, letting him keep pace on longer walks without tiring. He began learning to swim in the cold rivers nearby, awkwardly at first, and started helping sort and dry foraged herbs by season. This was also when he first started helping at the cook fire, mostly just tending the coals and turning spitted meat, absorbing more about how food was prepared than anyone realized at the time.

**Skills gained:**
- Perception 1
- Running 1
- Swimming 1
- Interest: Herbalism 1
- Cooking 1

**Physical development:** Endurance and strength ahead of age peers, keeps pace on long walks.

## Year 5
This was the year he began to understand, on his own and without anyone telling him directly, that he was different from the people raising him. Not because anyone said so, but because he was paying close attention to how the forest behaved around him compared to how it behaved around everyone else. Plants seemed to lean his way. Rain seemed to follow him more often than coincidence should allow. He finally asked Bracken outright whether he was human. Bracken told him yes -- meaning the shape he wore, the life he was living among them -- and Alpha accepted the answer at face value, though some part of him already knew that wasn't the whole truth. Physically he had grown noticeably dense for his size, heavier than he looked, and strong enough to carry loads that should have been too much for a child his age. He also started learning basic knots for securing shelter lines and food stores, and began helping track the seasonal migration of local game as part of the circle's planning. In the evenings Bracken started telling him the circle's own oral history, how the group had formed, who had come before, and the older, half-remembered stories about the awakening of magic itself that the circle's founders had lived through. Alongside the nature-reverence at the heart of everything they did, the circle also held onto an old tenet from further back in their lineage, something closer to scripture than any druidic teaching, the plain idea that you loved your neighbor as yourself and helped whoever crossed your path in need, whether or not they were one of your own. Bracken treated this as no less central to who they were than their bond with the land itself.

## Year 6
He took on real responsibilities in the circle this year, tending the fires, hauling water, learning which plants could heal a wound and which would make things worse. This was also the year he started spending real time with the circle's other children close to his own age, particularly **Fen** and **Tansy**, and the three of them became something like a proper pack, inseparable through chores, games, and mischief in roughly equal measure. He also began learning to chop and split wood with a hand axe, clumsy at first but quickly finding a natural rhythm for the swing. This was also when the circle began teaching him their runic system, an old set of carved symbols used to mark territory, ward camps, and record which plants and paths were safe. He noticed, almost by accident, that the fires he tended seemed to respond to his mood, burning higher when he was excited and settling low when he was calm. Rather than mention it right away, he spent weeks quietly testing the idea on his own before finally bringing it to Bracken, who took the news with a knowing nod rather than any real surprise. His coordination continued to sharpen, and hauling water and firewood daily built real muscle onto an already dense frame. He also started learning to start fires without matches, using flint and friction methods the circle still relied on, and took on more real responsibility at the cook fire, learning to season and time a meal rather than just tend the coals.

**Skills gained:**

## Year 7
He began joining the circle's hunters this year, learning to track, move quietly, and take a life cleanly when it was needed. The circle made sure he understood early that this wasn't cruelty, it was the plain fact of how the forest sustained itself, one life feeding another in an endless turn, and he took the lesson seriously rather than flinching from it. He spent the rest of his time learning the forest on its own terms, memorizing every notable tree, water source, and animal trail within a day's walk of camp. He began to notice that what he felt around him wasn't the same as what the others described feeling, and rather than finding that unsettling, he simply folded it into how he understood himself. Covering that much ground on foot built up real stamina, and he could now match the pace of adult circle members on long treks without falling behind. He also began learning to skin and process small game properly, and started picking up basic archery with a child-sized bow built for him by one of the hunters. Alongside land hunting he started fishing the rivers near camp with the rest of the circle, learning to read where fish gathered and how to build simple weirs and traps for them. The hunters also taught him a set of hand signals for staying silent while stalking game, something he picked up quickly given how naturally quiet he already moved. His drumming improved enough that he started playing at the circle's small seasonal gatherings rather than just practicing alone.

## Year 8
This was the year he first noticed he could deliberately draw on what he'd always felt passively. He had taken to sitting alone in the evenings, still and quiet, simply paying attention to the currents of energy he could feel moving through the ground and the air around him. During one of these sessions the fires across the entire camp flared at once without anyone touching them, the direct result of him focusing that attention harder than he ever had before. No one was hurt, but the moment stopped everyone cold. Bracken put a hand on his shoulder and said nothing at all. Alpha spent the following three days alone in the forest, quiet and still, trying to understand exactly what he had done and how he had done it, facing the fear of it directly rather than running from it.

What he came to understand, slowly and entirely on his own, was that the energy moved through him in two different ways depending on how he reached for it. Sometimes it stayed inside him, sharpening his body, quickening his reflexes, hardening his skin against the cold, an energy that lived in him and worked through him rather than leaving him at all. Other times it moved out and away from him entirely, taking shape somewhere past his own body, striking a target or lighting a space he could point to but not touch. He had no words yet for adept or magician or mystic adept. He only understood it as two directions the same current could flow, inward and outward, and that he seemed to be able to choose which way it went. In the weeks after, still shaken but curious, he began sparring lightly with wooden practice sticks with some of the younger adults, and kept working on his archery and tracking alongside it. Around this time one of the circle's healers started teaching him real first aid, how to clean and bind a wound, set a simple fracture, and recognize when something was beyond what the circle could treat on its own, practical knowledge that came up often given how many small injuries a life like theirs produced. Bracken also started teaching him more of the world outside the Peninsula during this stretch, basic knowledge of the UCAS, the corporations, and how the Sixth World had reshaped itself since the Awakening.

**Physical development:** First experience of genuine physical exhaustion, tied directly to the discharge.

## Year 9
He began deliberately exploring what he was rather than simply reacting to it as it happened. This wasn't magic in any formal or structured sense, just long stretches of sitting with the energy he had always felt and paying attention to its shape and texture. Around this time Wren, the circle's rune-keeper, started teaching him the deeper meanings behind the symbols he had already learned to recognize, explaining that certain runes weren't just markers but were believed to hold a sliver of the forces they represented. He discovered he could feel ley lines running beneath the ground and, without telling anyone where he was going, followed one for two full days. Partway through that trip he fell hard from a tall tree he'd been climbing to get his bearings, snapping his leg on the way down in a way that should have ended the journey right there. Instead something in him reacted on pure instinct, the same inward pull he'd been quietly practicing for months, and by the time the worst of the shock passed the bone had already begun knitting itself back together, faster and cleaner than it had any right to. He finished the two days on a leg that should have kept him pinned to the forest floor. When he came back Bracken was waiting for him, asked where he had gone, and listened carefully as Alpha tried to describe what he had felt along the way, including the fall and what had happened afterward. Bracken told him it sounded right. He also spent a good part of this year learning to build and mend simple shelters, lashing branches and bark into weatherproof lean-tos, and started helping deliver messages and small goods between the circle and a handful of trusted contacts on the edge of the wilderness. His cooking improved to the point where he was trusted to prepare meals for the whole circle unsupervised on occasion, and he picked up a simple wooden flute one of the members had carved, adding it to his drum practice.

## Year 10
Bracken finally told him everything the circle knew, which amounted to less than Alpha had hoped but more than he expected. They had felt what he was the instant they found him. They had chosen to raise him anyway, without hesitation, because they believed he was something the world -- or something beyond it -- had made deliberately rather than something that had simply happened. They didn't have the full names for his parents then, but they knew enough: he was not born of ordinary people. Alpha asked, plainly, whether his parents had been human. Bracken said no. It was the first time anyone had said it to him so directly, and he sat with that answer for a long time before he said anything else. (What the circle would eventually understand more clearly -- son of a living nexus and a goddess -- was already the shape of the truth Bracken was pointing at.) By this point his physical growth had leveled out somewhat compared to the earlier years, but he remained visibly stronger and more resilient than any child his age had reason to be. He spent much of the rest of the year deepening his archery and tracking, taking his first solo overnight hunts under light supervision. The first two ended in nothing, a deer he lost after misjudging the wind and a rabbit that simply outmaneuvered him in brush too thick to loose an arrow through, and he came back both times frustrated and empty-handed while Fen and Tansy teased him gently for it. He kept at it rather than sulking, and continued his rune studies alongside the keeper. Around this time the hunter who had built his first bow started teaching him how to maintain and eventually build his own, selecting the right wood, stringing it properly, and fletching his own arrows rather than relying on someone else's work. He also began learning to read the night sky in earnest, picking out the seasonal stars the circle used for orientation on nights when cloud cover made his own magnetic sense less useful to explain casually to others. Bracken also began teaching him more formally about the history of magic's return to the world and how different traditions, hermetic, shamanic, and otherwise, understood the same forces in different ways.

**Physical development:** Growth stabilizes but strength and resilience remain well above what is normal for his age.

## Year 11

He got good at it faster than expected, understanding almost instinctively how a frightened animal would move through unfamiliar terrain. Alongside that he kept working with the hand axe, moving from simple wood splitting to shaping his own tool handles and stakes. His body kept filling out, broad through the shoulders in a way that made the older circle members joke he'd out-carry all of them within a few years. He also began learning basic cordage-making from plant fiber, and picked up simple star navigation for nights when cloud cover made his usual magnetic sense less reliable to explain to others. His cooking grew more ambitious, experimenting with preserving meat and foraged food for the leaner months, and he kept up his music practice, now confident enough on both drum and flute to lead simple songs at gatherings himself.

## Year 12
He killed his first large animal alone this year, a deer he had tracked for two full days using the same patience he'd learned tracing ley lines years earlier. He field dressed it under an older hunter's supervision and didn't flinch through any of it. That night the circle held a small, quiet acknowledgment of the moment, not a ceremony exactly, just an evening where everyone made a point of eating together, and Alpha cooked the meal himself for the first time at that scale. He also started learning basic woodworking beyond tool handles, carving simple bowls and utensils, and picked up the rudiments of hide tanning from the animals he and the others brought in, work he kept refining in the years afterward until he could reliably turn his own kills into usable clothing and cordage. Alongside this he kept sparring regularly, moving from practice sticks to blunted practice blades under supervision, and started paying closer attention to how the older hunters read incoming weather from the sky and the behavior of animals, picking up the habit of doing the same himself. Bracken also started walking him through the broader history of the Sixth World in earnest this year, the corporate court system, the sovereign nations carved out after the Awakening, and where a place like the Olympic Peninsula fit into all of it.

## Year 13
This was the year his voice started changing and his growth accelerated noticeably, putting on both height and muscle in a way that outpaced every other adolescent in the circle. He leaned into the physical work that came with it, taking over some of the heavier labor around camp without being asked. He also started sparring more seriously with the older members using wooden practice weapons, picking up the basics of blocking and striking faster than his teachers expected, though nobody was entirely sure how much of that was training and how much was simply what he was. This was also the year one of the circle's oldest members, a quiet woman who had helped raise him since infancy alongside Bracken, passed peacefully in her sleep after a long decline nobody had hidden from him. The circle buried her the old way, wrapped plainly and returned to the earth without embalming or ceremony beyond what the moment itself demanded, and Bracken walked him through every part of it personally, digging the grave, preparing the body, saying what needed saying. It was the first time anyone close to him had died, and he grieved plainly and without shame, the way the rest of the circle did. Alongside combat training he continued deepening his herbalism, learning to prepare simple poultices and teas for common ailments, and began assisting more directly in trap and shelter construction for the whole camp rather than just himself. His music became more personal this year, less about performing for the group and more about working through his own feelings on the drum and flute alone in the evenings, and he started telling stories of his own around the fire as well, at first just retellings of the circle's oral history but gradually his own versions, shaped by how he actually understood the world. He also kept up fishing and climbing whenever the terrain called for it, both becoming second nature alongside everything else, and spent much of his free time that year with Fen and Tansy, the three of them old enough now to take on real shared responsibilities together rather than just playing.

## Year 14
He began building more elaborate traps this year, deadfalls and pit snares meant for larger game, and started teaching some of the younger circle children the basics of what he knew. Teaching forced him to actually articulate things he'd only ever done by feel, and it sharpened his own understanding in the process. This was also the year the circle's research into what he was reached a real turning point. Ever since his first uncontrolled discharge at age eight, Wren and two other circle elders with real magical theory background had been quietly compiling everything they knew and could learn about adepts, magicians, and the rarer mystic adepts who could do both, treating Alpha's existence as a subject worth genuine scholarship rather than just a mystery to live alongside. Six years into that effort, they sat him down and explained, in real technical terms for the first time, what a Mystic Adept actually was, and helped him understand that the inward and outward flows he'd been feeling since childhood had names and structure behind them. He also began working more directly with Wren on ley line theory, since the circle had spent years quietly mapping the lines running under their part of the Peninsula. Alongside this he kept refining his archery to the point where he could hit moving targets reliably at range, and continued sparring in both blades and unarmed styles. Bracken began teaching him practical knowledge of the awakened world's underside as well, street terminology, the rough shape of the shadow economy, and why a circle like theirs stayed hidden from it rather than engaging with it.

## Year 15
He started spending more time alone this year, days at a stretch away from camp, hunting and tracking and simply existing in the deeper parts of the forest without anyone checking on him. The circle let him go without argument, trusting both his skill and his judgment by this point. He came back from these trips more capable each time, better at reading weather before it turned, better at finding water in dry stretches, better at moving through the forest as though he belonged to it rather than simply surviving in it. This was also the year the circle's research group finished something they had been quietly building for years without telling him, a small ritual site at the convergence of two ley lines they had mapped, meant to serve as a stable place for exactly the kind of deep magical work Alpha was ready to begin attempting. They also shared with him, for the first time, their working theories on what kind of Mentor Spirit, if any, might be drawn to something like him, based on years of careful research into how such bonds tended to form. In the course of that research, they also noticed something else entirely: water, earth, and beast spirits summoned nearby seemed to take a real interest in him whenever he was present, more curious than wary, in stark contrast to the unease he seemed to provoke in spirits of a darker or more corrupted nature. Nobody had an explanation for it. It simply was. Bracken began quietly preparing him for the idea that he might not stay with the circle forever. He also picked up basic knowledge of local roads, trails, and the handful of settlements near the Peninsula's edge, in case he ever needed to navigate beyond the deep forest, and continued refining his cooking with whatever he could forage or catch on these solo trips, no longer dependent on the circle's stores at all.

## Year 16
He began pushing his solo trips further from camp this year, sometimes gone for a week or more, following ley lines to their source or simply walking until something about the land told him to stop. On one of these trips he found himself standing over a stretch of forest that had been quietly poisoned by runoff from a corporate logging operation miles away, the ground wrong in a way he could feel before he could see it. Something in him reacted before he understood why, and by the time he came back to himself the immediate area had been stripped clean of the contamination, the cost of which left him weak and disoriented for two full days afterward. Bracken didn't scold him for the risk. Bracken asked him what it had felt like to do that, and listened carefully to the answer. In the aftermath he threw himself back into training, sharpening his archery and blade work as though physical mastery might help him understand what had happened. He also began asking Bracken more pointed questions about the corporations and their history, wanting to understand exactly what kind of thing had caused the damage he'd felt.

## Year 17
Early this year, deep in a stretch of forest well beyond the circle's usual range, Alpha came across a woman lying badly hurt, unconscious and bleeding, a leg caught and torn in a steel trap clearly never meant for anything human, faint traces of poison in a shallow cut along her side, clearly not from anywhere near the Peninsula, unmistakably elf despite the ring through one eyebrow and the long, practical ponytail that no court back home would ever have approved of, her coat armored and real rather than decorative, the kind of gear that had actually been used rather than shown off. Pieced together later from what gear was found near her, she'd come across a corporate-funded paracritter poaching operation working the deep Peninsula on a real scale, trap lines and poisoned bait spread across a significant stretch of territory, and hadn't been able to look away from what she found, animals held and killed in conditions that turned her stomach. She'd moved against the operation directly, fighting through trap lines and armed poachers both, taking a blade or a snare's teeth to the leg and enough poison into her system through a shallow wound to drop her before she could finish what she started. Whatever happened in the fight, she came out of it running, covering real distance through unfamiliar terrain well beyond where the operation itself was based, far enough that Alpha, despite knowing his own territory like the back of his hand, had never once crossed anywhere near it, until the blood loss and poison finally caught up to her and put her down a long way downriver from wherever it had actually happened. Everything the circle had ever taught him, the reverence for what needed protecting and the older, plainer tenet of loving your neighbor and helping whoever crossed your path in need, moved him before he'd even finished thinking it through. He carried her back toward camp himself, and Bracken and the circle's healers set what they could with First Aid, Detox, and what safe healing Alpha could offer.

Her broken bones and surface wounds mended within weeks under the circle's care, faster than they should have thanks to Alpha's own instinctive healing touch, the poison purged from her system with Detox before it could do worse. But somewhere in the fight itself, a fall, a blow from a poacher she never saw coming, or simply her head striking something hard as she finally went down, left something else behind entirely. She woke knowing her first name, Elma, and little else, no surname, no clear memory of who she'd been or what she'd been doing in the forest, just fragments that surfaced unpredictably, a flash of a city street, a reflex that moved her hand like she was reaching for something that wasn't there, a word in a language none of the circle recognized muttered in her sleep. The circle took her in without hesitation, the same way they'd taken Alpha in eighteen years earlier, and let her heal on her own time. Alpha was sixteen when he found her. The year that followed -- his seventeenth -- would be the bulk of the quiet life they built together under the trees before anything else broke open.

He started actively training in hand to hand combat this year, not out of any particular need but because the circle believed a person capable of what he was capable of should also know how to control himself under pressure. He took to it seriously, sparring almost daily with whichever adult had time, and it was clear by the end of the year that his raw physical capability had outpaced anything training alone could account for. This was also the year the research group judged him ready for a real attempt at Initiation, using the ley line convergence site they had built specifically for this purpose. The ritual itself was unlike anything in their existing theory, since nothing about Alpha followed the normal pattern, but with a decade of collective preparation behind it, the attempt succeeded. He came out of it with a clearer, more deliberate command over the line between his inward and outward expressions of energy than any untrained adept his age would have, plus the first hints of an actual metamagic discipline the circle helped him recognize as something like Centering, using his craft and physical focus to soften the drain of pushing his own limits. He also began working more independently on his runic knowledge, experimenting quietly with carving small runes into pieces of wood and stone just to see what, if anything, would happen. Mostly nothing did. Occasionally something small did. Alongside this he refined his trapping and hunting further, able now to feed himself indefinitely without any support from the circle at all, and spent long evenings on drum and flute, music having become as natural to him as breathing. Through all of it Elma was present -- recovering, learning the camp, sitting near enough that he kept finding reasons to check on her -- the quiet center of a year that would have looked very different without her.

## Year 18
Elma was already well into her second year with the circle by then -- body long since healed, memory still returning only in scattered, unreliable pieces. Alpha spent more time with her than with anyone else in the circle that year, partly because Bracken asked him to, since he was the one who'd found her and the one whose healing had brought her back from the worst of it, and partly because he wanted to. He taught her the parts of the forest he knew like his own hands, and in exchange, whatever she could recall in a given week, she taught him fragments of the world she'd come from, half-remembered protocols, the shape of a city skyline she couldn't place, the feeling of chrome and static she associated with something called the Matrix without being able to explain what it actually was. Neither of them had solid ground to stand on when it came to the wider Sixth World, and that shared not-knowing became its own kind of closeness. Somewhere across the roughly year and a half since he'd carried her in, without either of them naming it out loud, what they felt for each other stopped being simple gratitude and became something neither of them had a framework for either.

Late in the year, close to the day he turned eighteen, they kissed for the first time, and something in her broke open that roughly a year and a half of gentle recovery hadn't managed to touch. It came back all at once and without warning, her real name in full, the job that had gone bad, the poaching operation, the team she'd been running with, the life waiting for her outside the trees, and underneath all of it, a childhood in the elven nation she'd walked away from, a family that had expected perfection from her and gotten someone who kept the ponytail and put a ring through her own eyebrow instead. She came back to herself gasping, gripping his arms, and it took her long minutes to separate the flood of who she actually was from the quiet, half-formed person she'd been for the circle since early the previous year. When she finally could speak again, she told him everything, plainly and without softening any of it, corporations, the Matrix, cities, the kind of work people like her did to survive in the spaces between all of it, and the court she'd left behind on purpose long before any of this happened. She was a shadowrunner, part decker and part something magical he still didn't fully have a name for, elven nobility by blood and a deliberate refusal of everything that was supposed to mean, and she had unfinished business she couldn't leave behind now that she remembered it existed.

He brought it to Bracken plainly, the way he brought everything to him, and Bracken didn't argue. Bracken sat him down and told him the circle had given him everything they had to give, and that whatever came next for him would happen somewhere beyond the reach of the Olympic Peninsula. He didn't frame it as being sent away. He framed it as the natural end of one part of his life and the beginning of another, the same way a season turns without any drama attached to it. Before he left, the research group gave him everything they had gathered over the past ten years, written and recorded as carefully as their limited resources allowed, so that whatever came next for him didn't have to start from nothing. He left the circle on his own terms, walking out under his own power, carrying nothing but what he had learned and what he had always been, and left with Elma not long after, the first real thread connecting him to the world he was walking into. In his final months with the circle he pushed his training harder than ever, refining everything he had built over the past seventeen years into something that finally felt whole, and spent his last nights around the fire cooking for the people who raised him and playing the songs they had taught him as a child right back to them. Fen and Tansy walked with him to the edge of the territory the morning he left, none of them quite finding the right words for goodbye, and settled instead for the kind of silence that says everything anyway.
